1. Executive summary

Indians already use voice in WhatsApp, Google Assistant, and voice search. On ChatGPT mobile, the blocker for working professionals is not “where is the mic?” — most have tried voice. The blocker is control: the transcript can feel committed before they read and fix it.

Focus segment: working professionals in India on ChatGPT mobile (daily use, high-stakes work prompts, subscription upside).

Research signal (directional, n=10 in segment): 70% say typing feels safer; 50% want check-and-fix before send; 80% use voice on WhatsApp; none rated ChatGPT voice above Google/Gemini.

Solution chosen: Voice Draft Mode — speech lands in an editable draft; nothing sends until the user taps Send. Prototype: Figma Make site.

North-star metric: voice prompt completion rate (mic session → Send), with edit rate and 7-day repeat as supporting metrics.

2. Milestone 1 — Market research

Full presentation: ChatGPT Voice Adaption amongst Indian Audience.pdf

Landscape (India)

  • Mobile-first internet; voice is a normal input channel.
  • English often used for work; Hindi/regional for social — recognition matters long-term, but voice habit already exists elsewhere.
  • ChatGPT mobile competes with Gemini Live on conversational feel.

Initial segments

  1. Non-English / vernacular-first users
  2. Tier-2 & Tier-3 mobile-first users
  3. WhatsApp voice-note natives
  4. Students & young learners
  5. Metro English-first professionals → deep dive in Milestone 2

Competitive UX patterns

ProductPattern
WhatsAppRecord → listen → send or re-record
Gemini LiveInterrupt and correct mid-stream
Google AssistantShort commands; less long-form prompting
ChatGPT mobileMic → transcript → feels immediate

Metrics explored

Weekly Successful Voice Users (WSVU), discovery rate, voice start rate, task success, repeat usage.

Sources (public): Reuters, DataReportal, Google for India, Google–KPMG Indian Languages, IAMAI–Kantar, ASER, OpenAI Help Center, Exchange4Media — see Milestone 1 PDF for citations.

Insights — quantitative & qualitative

Methods: Survey n=16 (10 in segment); 3 follow-up interviews + open-text; target 30+ for production validation.

70%Typing feels safer
50%Check-and-fix would help
80%Voice on WhatsApp

Hypotheses tested: Discoverability (weak) · Control (strong) · Accent (mixed) · Privacy (moderate) · Habit (strong).

  • Commit anxiety — wrong word becomes the prompt before fix.
  • WhatsApp mental model: listen before send; ChatGPT feels send-first.
  • Gemini: interrupt mid-sentence; ChatGPT composer feels one-shot.

4. Milestone 3 — Prioritisation, metrics & growth

Standalone 3-pager PDF: Milestone3_VoiceDraftMode_3Pager.pdf

Three directions & trade-offs

DirectionImpactEffort (2 wk)Decision
A. Voice Draft ModeHigh — adds reviewMediumBuild
B. Auto-organize / STAR guideMediumMedium–highLater
C. Recognition / HinglishHigh long-termVery highOut

User flow

  1. Mic visible in composer.
  2. Tap mic → speak → stop.
  3. Transcript in dashed draft (not sent).
  4. Tap word to edit; optional low-confidence underline.
  5. User taps Send → message enters thread.
  6. Discard returns to keyboard without sending.

Success metrics

MetricRole
Voice prompt completion rateNorth star (mic → Send)
Draft-to-send rateReview step completes
Edit rate on draftsProves review is used
7-day repeat voice useHabit
Typing share on 50+ char promptsGuardrail

Launch & growth

  • Experiment: 50/50 mobile, India + English, 2 weeks.
  • Kill rule: draft-to-send below control and edit rate < 15% → stop.
  • Growth: persistent mic; one first-run line; no badge spam; measure repeat not taps alone.

Non-goals (V1)

Advanced Voice Mode · new ASR programme · desktop/web · push-only “try voice” · auto-structure (phase 2 in prototype, labeled).
